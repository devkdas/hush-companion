# Corporate outreach

This guide helps introduce Hush Companion to companies without overstating what the current open-source repository can do.

## Current positioning

Use this description in early outreach:

> Hush Companion is an open-source, voice-first AI companion for reflection, conversation practice, learning, and everyday workday resets.

The current repository is a self-hosted prototype. It is not an enterprise-ready wellness platform and does not currently provide accounts, tenant separation, employer dashboards, SSO, compliance certifications, or a managed hosted service. Be clear about that in conversations with prospective customers.

Repository: <https://github.com/devkdas/hush-companion>

## Who to contact

Choose the recipient based on the problem you are exploring:

### Workplace wellness and employee experience

- Head of People
- Chief People Officer
- HR Director
- Employee Benefits Manager
- Workplace Wellness or Wellbeing Lead
- Employee Experience Manager
- Learning and Development Manager

### Innovation and technology pilots

- Innovation Program Manager
- AI Program Manager
- Digital Transformation Lead
- CTO or CIO at a small company
- VP of Engineering
- IT Director

### Smaller companies

For a company with fewer than a few hundred employees, the founder, CEO, COO, Head of People, or HR lead may be the most appropriate first contact.

Do not send the same message to every role. A People leader is more likely to care about employee experience and privacy; a technology leader is more likely to care about self-hosting, deployment, and integration.

## Where to find business email addresses

Use legitimate, public, or permission-based sources:

1. **Company website** — look at the Contact, About, Leadership, People, Partnerships, or Press pages.
2. **LinkedIn** — identify the relevant role first, then use the contact method the person or company publicly provides. A short connection message can be better than guessing an address.
3. **Company email pattern** — if the company publicly lists several addresses, you may infer a pattern such as `first.last@company.com`, but verify it before sending and do not use it for bulk mail.
4. **Warm introductions** — ask a former colleague, customer, founder, accelerator, or professional contact for an introduction.
5. **Professional directories and event pages** — use addresses that the person or organization has intentionally published for business contact.
6. **A company contact form** — use it when no relevant public email is available.

Avoid buying scraped lists, harvesting personal addresses, or sending mass untargeted campaigns. Respect each person's privacy, applicable email laws, and unsubscribe or opt-out requests. Check the rules that apply to your location and the recipient's location before sending commercial outreach.

## First email

Keep the first message short, specific, and easy to answer. Replace the bracketed text before sending.

### Subject options

- Exploring a private voice wellness pilot at [Company]
- Open-source voice companion for [Company]'s wellbeing initiatives
- A small idea for [Company]'s employee experience team
- Could Hush Companion support a private workplace pilot?

## Follow-up message

Send one brief follow-up after about five to seven business days. Do not continue if the person opts out.

```text
Subject: Re: Exploring a private voice wellness pilot at [Company]

Hi [Name],

Just following up on my note about Hush Companion. I’m looking for early feedback, not asking for a purchase, and I’m happy to send a short demo or close the loop if this is not relevant.

Would a brief conversation be useful, or is there someone else I should contact?

Best,
devkdas
```

## Outreach checklist

- Research the company and personalize one sentence.
- Contact one or two relevant people, not an entire department.
- Use the person's professional name and role.
- Link to the public repository and explain its current limitations.
- Never claim enterprise security, clinical benefit, guaranteed privacy, or an available hosted plan.
- Ask for feedback or a discovery conversation instead of asking for a purchase immediately.
- Keep a simple record of who was contacted and when.
- Honor opt-out requests and stop contacting people who decline.

## What to validate in conversations

Ask prospective teams:

- Which employee wellbeing or workday-reset problems are they trying to solve?
- Would self-hosting or a managed deployment be more useful?
- What data must never be visible to an employer?
- What authentication, retention, deletion, and audit controls are required?
- Who must approve an employee-facing AI pilot?
- What would make a small pilot successful?

Treat the answers as discovery feedback. Do not promise a corporate product, pricing, compliance certification, or employee analytics until those capabilities actually exist and have been reviewed.
